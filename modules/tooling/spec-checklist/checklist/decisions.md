# Decisions

The decisions a `deviates:` mark or an exception names, each as `- **D-<n>** <date> (<who>): <what was decided>`.

- **D-1** 2026-10-27 (owner): a client's assertion (`private_key_jwt`) names the issuer alone as its `aud` by default, since one naming the token endpoint may have been made for another authorization server; the token endpoint's URL is taken only from a client whose registration sets `client-assertion.accept-loose-audience`, and each registered client that sets it is warned of at every start.
- **D-2** 2026-10-29 (owner): the provider's own unregistered names keep no vendor prefix: authorize's resume parameters (`reauth`, `consent_denied`) and the error codes of its own steps (such as `step_up_required` and the second factor's `mfa_*`). They are read and answered at this provider's routes alone, and prefixing them would break 0.18's relying parties for no protection gained; the registered-name items are met where they bind, the token endpoint answering registered codes and token exchange's lifetime asked as `o3co_expires_in`. The rate-limit policy's `429 rate_limited`, and its `503` where it cannot count, come before any endpoint and go with no token type, parameter or grant, so rfc6749-8.5-a does not bind them.
- **D-3** 2026-10-29 (owner): an access token's sender binding and a client's authentication method are each client's, chosen by the operator: binding is required where `sender-binding.required` says so, and bearer tokens and `client_secret` stay allowed by default, as a client without DPoP or mutual TLS is the common web client; the security model's declared differences say so.
- **D-4** 2026-10-29 (owner): an access token's lifetime is the operator's (`tokens.access-token-lifetime`, 2s to 1d); the default stays one hour, as 0.18 had, and the runbook advises 5 to 15 minutes in front of a high-value resource server.
- **D-5** 2026-10-29 (owner): a refresh token family's end is fixed when it starts; there is no idle expiry, which C-3 adds in 0.21, since a default one would end families resting across the upgrade.
- **D-6** 2026-10-29 (owner): a confidential client's audience allowance is its allowance to introspect: it sees only tokens for the audiences it may be issued, with no introspection grant of its own.
- **D-7** 2026-10-29 (owner): a loopback `http` redirection or back channel URI is taken for any client, as a native app's callback (RFC 8252 §7.3), and a non-loopback `http` one for none, confidential clients included.
- **D-8** 2026-10-29 (owner): the End-User's pages are the deployment's: the provider offers logout (ending the session's access tokens and refresh families), the operator's subject revocation and the client's revocation endpoint, not a per-client revocation for the End-User, a candidate for a later minor.
- **D-9** 2026-10-29 (owner): a first-party client, run by the operator, is never asked consent for, `prompt=consent` included (authorize's card).
- **D-10** 2026-10-29 (owner): the names of `amr` elements are the provider's own, `recovery` and `email` among them, which 0.18's relying parties read; a DPoP challenge names no `algs` in 0.19, the binding kind's challenge taking it in 0.20.
- **D-11** 2026-10-29 (owner): these are the intended design, each held in its card: relying parties without a prior relationship come by Client ID Metadata Documents; `sub` is public alone; an error redirect carries RFC 9207's `iss`; a public client with a recorded consent and a live session is answered without interaction, held by the exact redirect URI and PKCE; no page warns before a redirect without TLS, loopback `http` and private-use schemes alone being taken (RFC 8252 §7.3, §8.4); frames are refused for every origin (`frame-ancestors 'none'`); certificate checks of the provider's own run above pkijs; a scope it does not know is refused `invalid_scope`; a logout whose hint is right is closed without asking, and one without a hint asks.
- **D-12** 2026-10-29 (owner): idp-google takes an `azp` naming another of the same project's clients, Google's cross-client identity, as its card says, rather than checking `azp` against its own `client-id`.
