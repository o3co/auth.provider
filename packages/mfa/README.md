# @o3co/auth-provider-mfa

Last updated: 2026-10-01

Multi-factor authentication for [`auth.provider`](../../README.md): a second factor after a password login, and a step-up when a relying party asks for one at `/authorize` — the package [the MFA ADR](../core/docs/adr/2026-09-25-multi-factor-authentication.md) designs (its D1).

> **Private.** The package is `"private": true`: built and tested in this workspace, never published, until the standalone template wires it (the ADR's build-order step 20). No release publishes a second-factor verification before the lock that bounds guessing exists (step 10). Nothing here is a supported API yet.

## Responsibility

**Role.** Everything MFA needs that is not a shared port: the `mfa` session requirement it contributes to core's session admission — what the MFA ADR called the coordinator — the browser API under `/session/mfa/*`, the factors that are not another package's, the key ring and what is sealed under it, lockout and audit. Core holds the ports the factors and the stores share; a factor from another package (WebAuthn's) reaches the requirement as an `mfaFactors` contribution.

**Owns:**

- the `mfa` session requirement — what MFA is to every consumer of a session, through core's admission — and `mfaModule`, which registers it as `sessionRequirements.mfa` and refuses the compositions that could not honour it;
- the login's MFA transaction: opened when the requirement interrupts a password login, and the `403` the login is answered with;
- the routes under `/session/mfa` — reading a transaction, a factor's challenge, a verification, a login's first binding, an enrollment from a signed-in session, the step-up of a first binding and the account-email proof — and the login a verified second factor, or a factor bound at a login, completes, with the audit events they emit;
- the enrollment witness's reads at login and its writes (the MFA ADR's D12, [`src/witness.mts`](src/witness.mts));
- the one first-binding gate (D24, D25, [`src/firstBinding.mts`](src/firstBinding.mts)) and the account-email proof ([`src/proof.mts`](src/proof.mts));
- MFA mail — the one place a code the provider issued is handed to the mail sender, and the masked address a page may show ([`src/mail.mts`](src/mail.mts)) — and the long code every 80-bit code is ([`src/codes.mts`](src/codes.mts));
- the TOTP factor (RFC 6238 on `node:crypto`), contributed as `mfaFactors.totp` by `mfaTotpFactorModule`, and the recovery-code factor, contributed as `mfaFactors.recovery_code` by `mfaRecoveryCodeFactorModule`;
- the sections its modules read — `mfa`, the MFA module's (the mode, the MFA page, the key ring, a transaction's life and attempts, the subject lock, recent MFA's window, the first binding's proof), `mfa-totp-factor`, the TOTP factor's module's, `mfa-recovery-code-factor`, the recovery-code factor's module's, and `mfa-email-factor`, the email factor's module's — their defaults ([`config/reference.conf`](config/reference.conf), exported as `@o3co/auth-provider-mfa/reference.conf`) and their refusals, the development sample key among them;
- sealing a factor's data and a ceremony's state under the key ring, and keyed digests for codes that are compared and never recovered — so that no factor holds a key;
- its testing entry, `@o3co/auth-provider-mfa/testing` ([`src/testing/index.mts`](src/testing/index.mts)): what another package's tests use of this one.

**Not yet here:** verifying a recovery code — a recovery code offered at a login is `503` until step 10, which also brings the subject lock and the weekly budget, so until then a guessable code is bounded per transaction (`mfa.maxAttemptsPerTransaction`) and per client address (`mfa.rateLimit.routes`) alone; the step-up of a subject who holds a counting factor — `POST /session/mfa/step-up` answers such a subject `404` until step 11, which also decides whether a factor bound in a session records a second factor on it; management, regenerating recovery codes and the operator reset (step 12).

**Does not own:**

- the `MfaFactor` contract, the `MfaFactorStore` and `MfaTransactionStore` ports and their adapters, the `mfaFactors` kind and `mfaFactorResolver` — core's (`packages/core/src/mfa/`), with Redis adapters in `@o3co/auth-provider-redis`;
- the WebAuthn factor — `@o3co/auth-provider-webauthn`'s `webauthnMfaFactorModule`, contributed as `mfaFactors.webauthn` and verified through these routes like any factor (the ADR's D4, F7);
- the pages: the login page's second step, the MFA page and the account page are the deployment's (D6).

**Why a separate package.** Core mounts no route but discovery and imports no sibling, and the MFA routes are browser routes that need the session package's CSRF guard and its login tail. A package also lets a deployment that opts out install none of it, and lets the factor code move on its own review cadence (D1).

## Configuration

Four sections, each its module's and named after it: `mfa`, the MFA module's; `mfa-totp-factor`, the TOTP factor's module's; `mfa-recovery-code-factor`, the recovery-code factor's; `mfa-email-factor`, the email factor's. Boot parses each with its module's schema before any factory runs, and hands it to the module's factories. Layer [`config/reference.conf`](config/reference.conf) between your `application.conf` and core's `reference.conf`, as for the WebAuthn package; it carries the defaults and the environment variables that override them. `mfaModule`, `mfaTotpFactorModule`, `mfaRecoveryCodeFactorModule` and `mfaEmailFactorModule` declare it as their sections' reference, so core's `moduleReferences(modules)` names it among the files to layer.

A key your `application.conf` sets shadows the substitution `reference.conf` makes for it; repeat the `${?VAR}` line after your value to let the environment override it again. `digits`, `period` and `window` are whole numbers, or their decimal digits as an environment variable carries them.

**The TOTP factor's old path.** `mfa.factors.totp` refuses the boot wherever `mfaTotpFactorModule` is installed (`config-path-relocated`), naming each key's path under `mfa-totp-factor` and the variable `reference.conf` binds it to (a value written at `mfa.factors.totp` itself names the section alone). Its variables, `MFA_TOTP_ENABLED` and `MFA_TOTP_ISSUER`, are bound to no setting and declared renamed on the factor's module (`section.renamedVariables`); `reference.conf` captures them and their new names in `renamed-variables`, so a composition that layers it is judged by what its resolution saw: exported alone, or beside `MFA_TOTP_FACTOR_ENABLED` / `MFA_TOTP_FACTOR_ISSUER` set to a different value, one refuses the boot (`environment-variable-renamed`), naming the new variable and its path; set to the same value as its new name, it boots. Set `MFA_TOTP_FACTOR_ENABLED` or `MFA_TOTP_FACTOR_ISSUER` instead.

**The MFA page's old path.** `endpoints.mfa.url` refuses the boot wherever `mfaModule` is installed (`config-path-relocated`), naming `mfa.page.url` and `MFA_PAGE_URL`. Its variable, `ENDPOINTS_MFA_URL`, is bound to no setting and declared renamed on the MFA module; `reference.conf` captures it and `MFA_PAGE_URL` in `renamed-variables`: exported alone, or beside `MFA_PAGE_URL` set to a different value, it refuses the boot (`environment-variable-renamed`); set to the same value, it boots. Set `MFA_PAGE_URL` instead.

**Key ids and rotation.** An entry written without an `id` — as `reference.conf`'s, which `MFA_ENCRYPTION_KEY` feeds — is named by its key's fingerprint: `k` and the first 16 characters of base64url(HMAC-SHA-256(key, `o3co:mfa:key-id`)), which tells nothing of the key. So a key changed in place leaves what the old one sealed `key_unavailable`, naming the old fingerprint — put that key back — never `unreadable`, which no key cures. An entry written with an `id` keeps it: never change its key in place. To rotate, write the ring in a deployment-owned layer: add the new key last as an entry of its own (a new `id`, or none), then move it first, and keep the old key until nothing sealed under it, and no digest naming it, is left. Opening data sealed under a key that is no longer first logs `mfa_factor_sealed_with_retired_key` (info, the key id) once per key id. Core's envelope seals with a random 96-bit AES-GCM nonce, and a factor's data is re-sealed at every use, so rotate each key well before 2^32 seals under it — the bound NIST SP 800-38D sets for random nonces.

**Refused**, each naming the key and quoting no key or id.

- Before any factory runs (`config-validation-failed`, the key's path with its section's): `mfa.mode` outside `off`, `optional` and `required`; an `mfa.page` that is not a section with a string `url`; in `mfa-totp-factor`, a parameter out of its range, an algorithm it does not take, an `enabled` that is no switch, or an issuer outside its rule; in `mfa-recovery-code-factor`, a key the section does not know, or a count outside its range; in `mfa-email-factor`, a key the section does not know (a subject line, a body or a send limit among them), or a code's life outside its range; a factor's section missing, because `reference.conf` is not layered; a section written as a value rather than a section of keys.
- By the MFA module's factory, a `RangeError` whose message starts with the key: `mfa.mode` `off` or unset, which a missing `mfa` section reads as; an empty ring or one without `MFA_ENCRYPTION_KEY`; a key that is not canonical base64 of 32 bytes; a duplicate id — a fingerprint that equals a written id among them — or one outside `A-Za-z0-9_-` (1 to 64 characters); one key listed twice, whatever ids it is written under; a transaction's life outside 60 to 1800 seconds, its attempts outside 2 to 10, recent MFA's window outside 60 to 3600 seconds, a subject's records outside 2 to 100, or a lock core's rule refuses; `mfa.enrollment.requireEmailProof` other than `when-mail`, `always` or `never`, and `always` with no mail sender wired, under which nobody could give the proof and so nobody could bind (D20).
- By the TOTP factor's factory: for a factor that is on and has no issuer written, an `oauth.jwt.issuer` with no host to default it to (naming `MFA_TOTP_FACTOR_ISSUER`).

`mfaConfigSchema` holds each shape and range of the `mfa` section: the mode, the page (a section with a string `url`, which may be left out), the transaction's life (60 to 1800 seconds) and attempts (2 to 10), and each lock field a positive whole number. Reading the settings makes the rest: the ring's refusals and the sample key's, where the keys are decoded and the environment is known, and core's `checkMfaLockoutPolicy` on how the lock's fields relate to each other (`threshold` at most `hardLimit`, `hardLimit` at most 100, `maxSeconds` at least `baseSeconds`, every duration within the Date range). The TOTP factor's module alone reads `mfa-totp-factor`; `mfaModule` reads every `mfa` key above and no factor's section, so a composition without the TOTP factor — its factors all another package's — is never refused over TOTP's.

**The development sample key.** [`MFA_DEVELOPMENT_SAMPLE_KEY`](src/config.mts) is a published key a development configuration may put in the ring in place of a key of its own. Everyone holds it, so it is refused — wherever it sits in the ring — when the environment the configuration was selected by is `production` or `staging`, when `NODE_ENV` is either (each read whatever its case and the whitespace around it), and under `core.deployment.mode = "multi"`, which the module reads from core's `deploymentMode` slot and never off the configuration — the rule the Redis federation stores' plaintext mode follows too. The environment reaches the refusal from the composition root (the standalone passes `CONFIG_ENV || NODE_ENV`) as `mfaModule({ environment })`. Where it is accepted, the module says so once at boot: `mfa_development_sample_key_in_use` (warn).

## Installing

Installed is on (the session-admission ADR's D7): a composition that wants no MFA installs none of this package, and an `mfa` section it writes is one no module reads. The standalone template reads `mfa.mode` itself before it chooses its modules, and declares `mfa` from it, until it installs this package at the MFA ADR's build-order step 20. One that wants it lists `mfaModules({ environment })` — the TOTP factor's module, the recovery-code factor's, the email factor's (off unless switched on, and refusing the boot when it is) and `mfaModule` — with an `MfaFactorStore` and an `MfaTransactionStore` (core's memory modules on one replica, `@o3co/auth-provider-redis`'s on several), the session package's login, its `loginCompletionModule` and its user-session store; sets `mfa.mode` to `optional` or `required`; and declares `"mfa"` in `core.sessionRequirements.expected`.

`mfaModule` requires `mfaFactorResolver`, `mfaFactorStore`, `mfaTransactionStore`, `userSessionStore`, `sessionRequirementResolver`, `csrfGuard` (every MFA POST runs it), `loginCompletion` (a verified second factor finishes the login through it: load the session package's `loginCompletionModule` beside `sessionModule`) and `deploymentMode` — core's, filled from `core.deployment.mode`, which the development sample key's refusal and the routes' limiter read — and reads `rateLimiter`, `auditSink` — its absence declared with `core.declaredAbsent = ["auditSink"]` — `subjectRevocation` — the boundary its routes' admission of a signed-in session reads, its absence declared with `oauth.revocation.subject = "unsupported"` — `logger`, `mailSender` — where the account-email proof and a factor's codes go — and `userRepository`, for the enrollment witness's write alone (`markMfaEnrolled`). It mounts `mfa-routes` (`MFA_ROUTES_ID`) at `/session/mfa`, after the session middleware (see [The routes](#the-routes)). It contributes `mfa.rateLimit.routes` as the budget of the `mfa` prefix (`MFA_RATE_LIMIT_PREFIX`), and registers `mfa.manage` (`MFA_ADMISSION_ACTIONS`, graded `credential_change`), the action its routes admit an enrollment from a signed-in session as. Without a `rateLimiter` the routes limit through a per-process one over that budget, which several replicas would each count apart: said once at warn (`mfa_rate_limiter_not_shared`) when `core.deployment.mode` is unset, silent under `single`. The boot is refused:

- `mfa.mode = "off"`, or unset, with the module installed: remove the module, or set the mode;
- once every factor has registered, for what a first binding's `hints.enrollable` would have to list, each `cause` carrying a `reason`: an enabled factor — this package's or another's — whose kind core's hint grammar refuses (`^[a-z][a-z0-9_-]{0,63}$`; `MfaFactorKindUnhintableError`, `mfa-factor-kind-unhintable`, naming the kind); more than 16 enabled counting factors, the most a hint list carries (`MfaTooManyFactorsError`, `mfa-too-many-factors`); and `mfa.mode = "required"` with no counting factor enabled, which nobody could meet (`MfaNoCountingFactorError`, `mfa-no-counting-factor`: it names the enabled factors that do not count, and asks for an installed counting factor's `enabled` key — the TOTP factor's, `mfa-totp-factor.enabled` (`MFA_TOTP_FACTOR_ENABLED`), where its module is installed);
- without a `userSessionStore`, a `csrfGuard` or a `loginCompletion`, at the requires-closure, naming the slot;
- under `core.deployment.mode = "multi"` without a `rateLimiter` (`replica-unsafe-adapter`): the routes' per-process limiter would count per replica;
- for a key ring, a transaction life, attempts, a lock or a first binding's proof the settings refuse (above), for `mfa.enrollment.requireEmailProof = "always"` without a `mailSender` (D20), and without `mfa.page.url`, the page a step-up starts on (`MFA_PAGE_URL`; `reference.conf` ships `/mfa`);
- by core, when the requirement's reach is not what the enabled factors reach, when `mfa` is not declared in `core.sessionRequirements.expected`, or when another installed requirement declares the second-factor authority too (`duplicate-second-factor-authority`).

**Switching `optional` to `required`.** A live password session of a user who holds no counting factor has no second factor to step up with: at its next action it is asked to log in again, and the login binds the user's first factor (the MFA ADR's F2 amendment).

A `userSessionStore` without `recordSecondFactor` (core's `supportsSecondFactorUpdate`) cannot record a step-up: the module boots, says so once at warn (`mfa_step_up_unsupported`, with the adapter's `kind`), and the requirement sends a session to log in again where it would step it up.

A directory without `markMfaEnrolled` (core's `supportsMfaEnrollmentWitness`), or none, cannot hold the enrollment witness: the module boots and says so once at warn (`mfa_enrollment_witness_unwritable`); D12's defence is then what the Store answers on `authenticate`. With `mfa.enrollment.requireEmailProof = "when-mail"` and no `mailSender`, a first binding asks for no proof: said once at warn (`mfa_first_binding_without_email_proof`) — notify account holders of `mfa.factor.enrolled` from the audit events.

## The `mfa` requirement

Registered as `sessionRequirements.mfa` ([`src/requirement.mts`](src/requirement.mts); the session-admission ADR's D6):

- **The second-factor authority** — the requirement declares it (`secondFactorAuthority`), which is what core weighs, never the name: core lets the authority alone reach and add a second factor's `amr` values and `mfaAt`, holds its completion of a login to a verified second factor, and binds it at boot to `mfaFactorResolver`, `mfaFactorStore` and `mfaTransactionStore`. The name `mfa` is this package's own.

- **Reach** — what a step-up can add to a session: each enabled factor's `amrValues`, and `mfa` when one of them adds it (TOTP: `otp` and `mfa`), read once — at boot, after every factor registered — and kept: core seals that reach and merges with it, and the requirement's own verdicts read it too. The page a step-up starts on is `mfa.page.url`; the one remediation the requirement declares is `mfa.step_up`, the step-up route's.
- **At each use** (`admit`), by the action's grade — the requirement names no action:
  - `use`: under `optional` every session is met. Under `required`, a session a cookie, a code or a link carries is met when its primary is a federation, or when a second factor was verified in it (`mfaAt`); a password session without one is stepped up to the MFA page when its subject may hold a counting factor to step up with — or unmet, when no factor is enabled, and sent to log in again when the session store cannot record a step-up — and sent to log in again when it holds none, where the login binds its first factor; a session whose primary cannot be told, or no live session, is sent to log in again.
  - `grants_nothing` — device verification's `device.lookup` and `device.deny`: met on any live session a cookie, a code or a link carries, whatever it is named, so a user refuses a phished device request without a step-up.
  - `credential_change` — an action that adds a way into the account: the link start (`session.link`), WebAuthn registration (`webauthn.register`), an enrollment from the account page (`mfa.manage`). A session a cookie, a code or a link carries is held — under `required` on top of the baseline, whose answer comes first, so the grade is never looser than `use`; under `optional` alone — to what the subject's factor records say: a record counts unless an installed factor of its kind declares it does not (recovery codes), so a kind no longer installed is presumed to count, and a store that cannot answer is an outage (`503`).
    - **A subject with a counting factor** needs **recent MFA** ([`isRecentMfa`](src/requirement.mts)): a second factor verified in the session within `mfa.manage.maxAgeSeconds` — the window's edge included, an `mfaAt` up to `DEFAULT_CLOCK_SKEW_MS` ahead of the clock read as now and one further ahead as not recent. Without it the session is stepped up to the MFA page — unmet when no factor is enabled, sent to log in again when the session store cannot record a step-up.
    - **A subject without one** is making its first binding (D12, D24), a passkey and a linked identity as much as a factor. What the session recorded of its login's `User` (`enrollmentFacts`, the view admission hands the requirement) is read first: a session that recorded nothing is sent to log in again — a fresh login records it, so a store of your own must round-trip it — and a witness that says the subject enrolled, or says nothing readable, beside no record that may count records `mfa.enrollment_state_inconsistent` and is `503`, never a first binding. Then the primary (`authTime` — a second factor verified in the session does not stand in for it) must be as recent, by the same rule, or the session is sent to log in again. Then the one gate ([below](#the-routes)) over the account's address as recorded and D25's flag: no proof asked is met; a proof asked is met only while one given in that same session through the step-up stands — `mfa.manage.maxAgeSeconds` from when it was given; a store answering an older one, the clock skew allowed, answers none — and stepped up to the MFA page otherwise; a proof nobody can give is stepped up and never met.
    - A session whose primary is neither a password nor a federation, and no live session, are sent to log in again.
    - The window bounds the admission, not what follows it: the link start is admitted, and its callback writes the identity up to the federation transaction's lifetime (10 minutes) later.
    - Hosts' clocks must agree within `DEFAULT_CLOCK_SKEW_MS`: `mfaAt` carries the clock of the replica that recorded it, so a replica whose clock runs ahead stretches recent MFA by its lead.
    - Until the step-up of a subject who holds a counting factor exists (`POST /session/mfa/step-up` answers such a subject `404`, below), that subject can link an identity, register a passkey or enroll another factor only within `mfa.manage.maxAgeSeconds` of a login's second factor; after that it is answered `403 step_up_required` and must sign in again — closed, never open.
  - A token is judged on its own `amr` whatever the grade, under `required`, and met under `optional`: a refresh token is judged on what it was issued with, whether or not it names a live session. A federation's is met; one carrying a factor's own second-factor value (`otp`, `hwk`, `swk`, `email`, `recovery` — never `mfa` alone) is met whatever its primary, so a passkey sign-in's `["hwk"]` token keeps refreshing; a password alone is unmet; anything else — no `amr` at all (a token issued before tokens carried one), an unknown value — is sent to log in again.
- **At a password login** (`admitPrimary`), the subject's factor records are listed — a store that cannot answer is `503`, with nothing written. When no record that may count stands (none, or recovery codes alone), the login's `User` must not say the subject enrolled: a witness `true`, or one that is neither `true`, `false` nor absent, records `mfa.enrollment_state_inconsistent` and is `503` under either mode — the factor store was lost, or the Store answers wrongly — never a first binding (D12). Any record at all interrupts the login for a second factor; a record this deployment cannot use (a retired kind, recovery codes alone) is never read as none. With no record, `optional` establishes the session as before, and `required` interrupts it for a first binding — when every counting factor refuses this user (`enrollable(user)`), the list is empty and each such login is said at warn (`mfa_enrollment_nothing_enrollable`, the kinds alone) — asking the account-email proof first where the gate does (below). A federated login is not asked (the federation callback does not consult admission in this release).

## The login's interruption

A password login the requirement interrupts is answered `403` once the express session is regenerated — left unauthenticated — and the login's MFA transaction opened, bound to the regenerated session (`binding: { kind: "session", id }`, which every later use compares whole); no session is written until the ceremony completes. The body is the closed shape core validates, and the page reads nothing else:

```json
{ "error": "mfa_required", "transaction": "<id>", "expires_in": 600 }
```

```json
{ "error": "mfa_enrollment_required", "transaction": "<id>", "expires_in": 600,
  "hints": { "enrollable": ["totp"], "email_proof": false } }
```

- `transaction` is 32 random bytes, base64url. It is not a bearer: every use compares the session it is bound to with the browser's, so the page keeps the cookie the `403` set. It travels only in request bodies and the `MFA-Transaction` request header — never in a URL.
- `expires_in` is `mfa.transactionTtlSeconds`; the transaction expires then, and the user starts again from the password.
- `mfa_required`: the subject holds a factor; the page asks for it.
- `mfa_enrollment_required` (`required`, no factor on record): `hints.enrollable` lists the counting factors this user may enroll, in registration order; `hints.email_proof` says whether the account-email proof comes first, and the transaction requires exactly what it says (the gate, under [The routes](#the-routes)).
- The `403` carries a fresh CSRF token, as a successful login does.

## The routes

Mounted at `/session/mfa` ([`src/routes.mts`](src/routes.mts), over the coordinator in [`src/coordinator.mts`](src/coordinator.mts)); the page is the deployment's (the MFA ADR's D6). The routes complete a login's transaction, and an `enroll` one a signed-in session opened — to add a factor, or to give the account-email proof a first binding in that session asks for.

- **Every answer** carries `Cache-Control: no-store`.
- **Every POST** sits behind the deployment's CSRF guard (`csrfGuard`: the session's double-submit token, or a same-origin `Origin` / `Referer`; refused `403 access_denied`), then the flood guard, `mfa.rateLimit.routes` per client address under `mfa:ip:<ip>` (`429 rate_limited`), before anything is read. Bodies are JSON or a form, parsed on these paths alone.
- **The transaction id** travels in the body's `transaction_id` or the `MFA-Transaction` header — the header alone on the `GET` — and never in a URL; two that disagree name none. A transaction bound to another browser, missing, malformed, spent, expired, or neither a login's nor an `enroll` one of the signed-in session presenting it — its `sid` and subject — is answered alike, spending nothing: `400 {"error":"invalid_request","error_description":"Unknown or expired MFA transaction"}`.
- **A signed-in session** is admitted before its transaction is read — to enroll, as `mfa.manage`; to give the proof or read its transaction, through the requirement's `mfa.step_up` remediation, which holds it to a live, unrevoked session alone — and its `User` is the one its cookie holds (`cookieSessionUser`). Not signed in, no longer live, or revoked: `401 {"error":"login_required","error_description":"Log in again"}`; a requirement's or a store's outage: `503`. A login's ceremony runs on a browser session that is not signed in yet, and admits nothing.
- **A factor** is named by `factor_id`, one of the subject's that an installed factor verifies; anything else is `400 {"error":"invalid_request","error_description":"Unknown second factor"}`, spending nothing.
- **An outage** — a store that cannot answer, or answers outside its port's contract (core's `readMfaAttemptReservation`, `isConsumedMfaTransaction`, `isMfaFactorUpdateWritten`), a factor whose data does not open, a factor that throws — is `503 {"error":"temporarily_unavailable","error_description":"MFA temporarily unavailable"}`, logged once at error; never a wrong code, never "no factor", never a verification.

**A verification**, in order: the bound read (`getBoundMfaTransaction`), after which only operations that carry the version it read, and `reserveAttempt` once it held; the factor's data opened (before an attempt is spent, so an outage spends none); an attempt reserved (`mfa.maxAttemptsPerTransaction`; past it the store ends the transaction and the answer is `401 mfa_invalid` with `attempts_remaining: 0`, audited `exhausted`) — so N codes sent at once spend N attempts; the factor's challenge taken, when it has one — kept state that does not open is `503`, the attempt spent; the proof checked (`401 mfa_invalid` and `mfa.verify.failure` with its `reason`), and the factor's `amrFor` held to its `amrValues` — non-empty, nothing it does not declare, else `503` as an unreadable factor; **the transaction consumed**, before anything else is written — so of N right proofs sent at once one completes and the rest are answered `400` as a spent transaction, and a verification that loses the transaction writes nothing to the factor; the factor's next state written by compare-and-set on its version, re-sealed under the ring's first key, with `lastUsedAt` — a lost compare-and-set reads the factor and checks the proof again, so a TOTP code used in two logins at once completes one (RFC 6238 §5.2); `mfa.verified`; then the login.

**The login's completion.** The verification's additions — the factor's `amr` values, `mfa` when the factor adds it, and `mfaAt` — go to core's `resumePrimary` with the continuation the transaction carried, and every other requirement not yet done in that login is asked. `establish` is written through the `loginCompletion` slot, the session recording `amr` `["pwd", "otp", "mfa"]` for a TOTP login (`["pwd", "hwk", "mfa"]` or `["pwd", "swk", "mfa"]` for a WebAuthn one) and `authentication.mfaAt`; another requirement's interruption is answered through it too (its ceremony on a regenerated session, its `403` with a fresh CSRF token). A requirement's store that cannot answer is `503` with admission's line; a session store that cannot is `503 {"error":"temporarily_unavailable","error_description":"Session store unavailable"}`, rolled back.

**The first binding** (F3, D24, D25; [`src/enrollment.mts`](src/enrollment.mts)). A login's transaction whose `enrollment` is not `none` binds one counting factor the login's `User` may enroll:

- **Refused, spending nothing:** a transaction that asks for a second factor (`400 {"error":"invalid_request","error_description":"No enrollment is open in this MFA transaction"}`) — so a subject whose only record does not open never binds; a kind that is not a counting factor this user may enroll (`400 … "Unknown second factor kind"`); a completion with no enrollment begun or one expired (`400 … "No enrollment is pending in this MFA transaction"`); a label a page cannot show (`400 … "Invalid label"`); while the account-email proof is owed, `403 {"error":"mfa_email_proof_required"}` — D25's flag is read again at each call, so a flag an operator reset set after the transaction opened makes it owe the proof; and once the subject holds any record — another session bound one — `401 login_required`. A factor that cannot start its enrollment (the WebAuthn factor for an account whose username is not well-formed text) is `503`, once at error (`mfa_factor_enrollment_unavailable`), and never falls back to another name.
- **The completion, in order:** an attempt reserved (`mfa.maxAttemptsPerTransaction`; the proof of possession is never held to the subject lock); the proof checked by the factor; its data sealed; **the transaction consumed** — of two completions of one transaction at once one binds, the other is answered as a spent transaction, and no factor is written twice; the factor written (`binding`: `email_proof` when the proof was given, else `password`); **the subject's records read again** — the binding stands only when they show its own record alone; another beside it (another login of the subject bound one at once), or its own gone (a reset removed it), and this one's is removed, trying three times, `mfa.first_binding_conflict` is recorded (the subject, the kind, and `removed`), and the answer is `401 login_required` — or `503` with `removed: false` and one `mfa_first_binding_factor_standing` line when the factor still stands (a re-read that fails removes it too, and is `503`, with the same line when it cannot); D25's flag cleared where the proof was given; the recovery codes written as one `recovery_code` record, each code only as a keyed digest; the enrollment witness marked; then the login resumes through `resumePrimary`, with the factor's `amr`, `mfa` and `mfaAt`, and is established or answered by another requirement.
- **What stands when a later write fails:** a factor that cannot be written is `503`, the transaction spent; recovery codes that cannot be written leave the binding standing and the answer says so — `"recovery_codes_issued": false` in place of `recovery_codes`, one `mfa_recovery_codes_unwritten` line — so the page can point to regenerating them (step 12); a witness that cannot be marked is one `mfa_enrollment_witness_unwritten` warning, healed at the next password login that verifies a counting factor. With the recovery-code factor off, the answer carries neither key. The codes are in this answer alone: when another requirement interrupts the resumed login (its `403` is the answer), core refuses the continuation (`401 login_required`) or the session store is out (`503`), the codes are written and not shown. The session a first binding establishes records the login's `User` as it was — not enrolled — until the next login.
- **At most one first binding.** Two logins of one subject that bind at once may both be dropped, each answered `401`: the users log in again. So whoever holds a user's password can keep racing the owner and hinder the owner's first binding — never gain a way in; the remedy is a password change, which is the Store's. The exclusion relies on the factor store keeping its write order: a write is visible to the read that follows it.

**An enrollment from the account page** (F4; [`src/enrollment.mts`](src/enrollment.mts)). `POST /session/mfa/enrollment {kind}` with no transaction admits the signed-in session as `mfa.manage` — the requirement's `credential_change` rule, [above](#the-mfa-requirement) — and opens an `enroll` transaction bound to the browser session, its `sid` and its subject, living `mfa.transactionTtlSeconds`: `enrollment: "required"` when the subject holds no record that may count, `"allowed"` beside one. `…/complete` admits the session again and compares the transaction's `sid` and subject with it, so a completion in another session, or once the requirement would step it up, binds nothing and spends nothing.

- **Another factor** — the subject holds one that may count — needs recent MFA (else `403 step_up_required`), binds by `mfa`, and is handed the subject's factors of its kind. Nothing beside a record that may count is ever a first binding, and a transaction keeps the binding it was opened for: a first binding's refuses any record found at its completion, and another factor's refuses a subject left with none that may count — each `409 {"error":"mfa_enrollment_conflict"}`, since the session stands and the page starts the enrollment again; so is a first binding dropped because another stood beside it at once.
- **A first counting factor** needs a sign-in within `mfa.manage.maxAgeSeconds` (else `401 login_required`) and the gate's proof where it asks one (else `403 step_up_required`, below); it binds by `email_proof` when the proof was given on its transaction or in the session, else by `password`, over zero records, with the one-first-binding check, D25's flag cleared by a proof, the recovery codes and the witness mark as at a login. A subject whose only records do not count (an email factor, say) is refused, `409 mfa_enrollment_conflict`.
- **The limit.** A subject holding `mfa.maxFactorsPerSubject` records is answered `409 {"error":"mfa_factor_limit"}` at the start and at the completion, before an attempt is spent. Completions made at once each read the records again once their factor is written, and one that finds them past the limit removes its own and is answered the same `409`, so the limit holds. One that cannot remove it — or cannot read the records again and cannot remove it — leaves the factor standing and usable: it is audited `mfa.factor.enrolled`, said once at error (`mfa_enrollment_factor_standing`), and answered `503`.
- **What `binding` says.** `password`: bound on the primary sign-in alone — a password, or a federation's; `email_proof`: after the account-email proof; `mfa`: beside a factor, with recent MFA.
- `GET /session/mfa/transaction` lists no factor for an `enroll` transaction: it verifies the account-email proof alone. A body and an `MFA-Transaction` header that name different transactions are `400`, and open none.
- The session is left as it was: a factor bound in it records no second factor on it.

**The step-up of a first binding** (the MFA ADR's F2 first-binding branch, D24). `POST /session/mfa/step-up` admits the session through the requirement's `mfa.step_up` remediation, then asks the requirement, as `mfa.manage`, what a first binding in it is answered: a witness that says the subject enrolled with no counting record, or a store's outage, is `503`; a sign-in older than `mfa.manage.maxAgeSeconds`, or a session that recorded no facts, `401 login_required`. A subject who holds a record that may count is answered `404`; another requirement's step-up is answered as that requirement's `403 step_up_required`; under `required`, a password session whose subject holds no counting factor never gets here — it is sent to log in again. Otherwise it answers an `enroll` transaction owing the account-email proof: the one `transaction_id` names, when it is the session's own first binding's and its proof is not met, or a new one. The proof is given on it through `POST /session/mfa/challenge` and `/verify` with `factor_id: "account-email"` (below), mailed to the address the session's login `User` carried; a verified proof is recorded for that session alone (`MfaTransactionStore.recordSessionEmailProof`), standing `mfa.manage.maxAgeSeconds`, and every first binding in the session is admitted on it while it stands. A proof lost with the store is given again. An `enroll` transaction takes the account-email proof alone: any other `factor_id` is `400 … "Unknown second factor"`.

**The page's contract.** A first binding's `403 step_up_required` — from the link start, WebAuthn registration or `POST /session/mfa/enrollment` — carries `"requirement":"mfa"` and `page`, the MFA page's URL. The page calls `POST /session/mfa/step-up`; with the transaction it answers, `POST /session/mfa/challenge` and `/verify` with `factor_id: "account-email"`; then returns to the page it came from, which starts the binding again. `404` there means the subject holds a counting factor: until step 11 it signs in again to have one verified. A proof nobody can give is refused at the challenge, `403 {"error":"mfa_email_proof_unavailable"}`: the account's address must be fixed in the Store.

**The gate** ([`src/firstBinding.mts`](src/firstBinding.mts)) is one function for every first binding — a first factor at a login, and, in a session, a first factor, passkey or linked identity — over what each caller hands it: `mfa.enrollment.requireEmailProof`, whether a `mailSender` is wired, the account's address as the session's enrollment facts say it (core's one reading: `none`, `address`, or `unreadable` — present, and not one address `normaliseMailAddress` reads), and D25's flag. `when-mail` asks for the proof when a sender is wired and the account has an address, or one it cannot read; `always` always; `never` never, whatever the address; the flag always. A proof asked for that nobody can give — no sender, no address, an address no proof can be sent to — is asked for and refused (`403`), never skipped, and said at warn at each login or admission that asks it (`mfa_email_proof_unprovable`, with the reason, never the address). At a login the proof is kept on the login's transaction; in a session, for that session alone, `mfa.manage.maxAgeSeconds`.

**The account-email proof** ([`src/proof.mts`](src/proof.mts)): `factor_id: "account-email"` on a transaction that owes it — a login's, or an `enroll` one the step-up answered — (`400 … "Unknown second factor"` on any other).

- `POST /session/mfa/challenge` mails a long code — 16 Crockford base32 characters, 80 bits — and answers `200 {"sent_to":"a***@example.com", "expires_in":<s>}`, audited `mfa.challenge.sent` with `kind: "account-email"`. It goes to the address the login's `User` carried — at a login, the password check's answer; in a session, the `User` the session's cookie holds — never a later read of the Store, which has no read by id, so the address is at most the transaction's life old, or the sign-in's in a session, which the step-up holds within `mfa.manage.maxAgeSeconds`. The code lives ten minutes, capped at the transaction's expiry; a resend replaces it. No sender, or no address: `403 {"error":"mfa_email_proof_unavailable"}`.
- `POST /session/mfa/verify` takes the code as it was shown, in either case, with or without hyphens: `200 {"email_proof":"verified"}`, audited `mfa.verified`. It spends the transaction's attempts — five wrong codes end the transaction — and never the subject lock. The code stands across attempts until it is replaced. On an `enroll` transaction a right code also records the proof for its session; a store that cannot record it is `503`, and the proof is given again.

**Mail** ([`src/mail.mts`](src/mail.mts)): a factor's login code, an email factor's enrollment code and the account-email proof go through `sendMfaMail` — the account's address compared with the digest a factor recorded before anything is written, the pending state kept with the digest of the address the code goes to and handed back to the verification, then the sender handed the purpose, the subject, that address, the code and its expiry, and its answer read through `mailSendOutcome` alone. A factor whose recorded address no longer matches the login's, or that holds none it can read, is `403 {"error":"mfa_factor_refused"}`, audited `mfa.email_address_mismatch` with the subject and the kind — nothing sent, nothing kept. The sender's limit is `429 {"error":"rate_limited"}`; its outage, or no sender wired, is `503` with one `mfa_mail_unavailable` line. Either clears the pending code; the transaction stands.

**Audit** (the MFA ADR's D28), each with `subject`, `ip`, `userAgent`, and `kind` and `purpose` in its details: `mfa.challenge.sent`; `mfa.verified`; `mfa.verify.failure`, with `reason` — `invalid`, `expired`, `replayed`, `malformed`, `sign_count_regression` or `exhausted` — and `factorId` when the factor names the factor the refusal concerns (the WebAuthn factor's `sign_count_regression`): the record id of one of the subject's factors of that kind, which the coordinator checks, else left out; `mfa.email_address_mismatch`; `mfa.factor.enrolled`, with `binding` (`password`, `email_proof`, or `mfa` beside another factor) and `by: "user"`, `purpose` `login` at a login and `enroll` from the account page; `mfa.recovery_codes.generated`, with `binding`, `by: "user"` and `regenerated: false`; `mfa.first_binding_conflict`, with `kind` and `removed` alone — a first binding dropped because another login of the subject bound one at once, which may be a password holder racing the owner; `removed: false`, a factor that could not be removed and may still stand, is for an operator (the runbook). The requirement records `mfa.enrollment_state_inconsistent`, with `witness` — `purpose: "login"` at a login, and `purpose: "session"` with the admitted `action` at a first binding in a session. None carries an address or a code.

**Logs** never carry the transaction id, a proof, a code, an address, a secret or a factor's data: `mfa_store_unavailable` (error: `route` — `step-up` among them — `store` — `mfa_transaction`, `mfa_factor`, `user_session`, `cookie_session` or an interrupting requirement's name — `step`, the error's projection; a session's proof's read and write are `sessionEmailProofAt` and `recordSessionEmailProof`), `mfa_factor_unreadable` (error: `route`, `kind`, `factorId`, `state` — `unreadable`, `key_unavailable` with the `keyId` to put back, `verification`, `challenge` when a pending challenge's kept state does not open, with the `keyId` when that key is the one missing, or `enrollment` when a first binding's pending enrollment does not open or its factor threw completing it), `mfa_factor_challenge_unavailable` (error: `kind`, `factorId`), `mfa_factor_enrollment_unavailable` (error: `kind`) — a factor's own failure, here and in `mfa_factor_unreadable`'s `verification` and `enrollment`, by its `name` and `code` alone, never its text, `mfa_mail_unavailable` (error: `route`, `purpose`, `kind`, `reason` — `outage` or `no_sender` — `cleared`, and the sender's failure by its `name`, `code` and `status` alone, never its text), `mfa_mail_refused_at_limit` (warn: `route`, `purpose`, `kind`, `cleared`), `mfa_email_proof_unprovable` (warn: `sub`, `reason` — `no_sender`, `no_address` or `unreadable_address`; at each login or first-binding admission that asks the proof), `mfa_recovery_codes_unwritten` (error: `sub`), `mfa_first_binding_factor_standing` (error: `sub`, `kind`), `mfa_enrollment_factor_standing` (error: `sub`, `kind` — another factor past the limit that could not be removed, standing), `mfa_enrollment_witness_unwritten` (warn: `sub`), `mfa_email_proof_flag_uncleared` (warn: `sub`), `mfa_login_not_resumed` (warn), `mfa_refusal_factor_id_dropped` (warn: `kind` — a factor refused naming, as the factor it concerns, an id that is none of the subject's factors of that kind; the refusal is audited without `factorId`, and the value is never logged), `mfa_login_cleanup_failed` (warn), `subject_session_index_write_failed` (error), at boot `mfa_rate_limiter_not_shared`, `mfa_enrollment_witness_unwritable` and `mfa_first_binding_without_email_proof` (warn), and the sealing's `mfa_factor_sealed_with_retired_key` and `mfa_digest_made_with_retired_key` (info, once per key id: a key still needed, by sealed data or by a stored digest).

The suites that pin these: `src/__tests__/routes.*.test.mts` (`routes.selfService.test.mts` and `routes.stepUp.test.mts` for a signed-in session), `requirement.firstBinding.test.mts`, `mail.test.mts`, `codes.test.mts`, `firstBinding.test.mts` and `recoveryCodeFactor.test.mts`.

## The TOTP factor

`mfaTotpFactorModule` ([`src/totp/module.mts`](src/totp/module.mts)) contributes the factor ([`src/totp/factor.mts`](src/totp/factor.mts)), built from its section, `mfa-totp-factor`; it answers `null` when the factor is switched off, which leaves the kind absent from `mfaFactorResolver`. It holds no state and reads no key.

- A verification adds `otp`, and `mfa` beside it (D14). The factor counts as MFA, and its six-to-eight-digit proof is guessable, so the subject lock applies to it (D21).
- A code is accepted at the steps `T - window` to `T + window`, and only when its step is after the factor's `lastUsedStep` — the same code twice, or an older code once a newer one was accepted, is refused as `replayed` (RFC 6238 §5.2). There is no drift resynchronisation (D22). HOTP and TOTP are pinned by RFC 4226 Appendix D's and RFC 6238 Appendix B's vectors.
- Each factor keeps the algorithm, digits and period it was enrolled with, so changing them in the configuration never breaks an enrollment; the window applies to every verification.
- Enrolling hands out a secret of the algorithm's output length (20, 32 or 64 bytes) in RFC 4648 base32 without padding, and the `otpauth://totp/<issuer>:<account>?secret=…&issuer=…&algorithm=…&digits=…&period=…` URI authenticator apps read, the account being the Store's username as it is — so where the username is an address, the label shows it, to the account's owner alone, in this answer and in the owner's authenticator app; an account without a well-formed username is refused, quoting nothing. The pending enrollment keeps the secret and its parameters alone, never the URI or an address; nothing the provider keeps — a factor's data, a pending state, an audit event, a log line — carries the address. The page renders it as a QR code (D6). The proof of possession binds the factor at the step it matched.
